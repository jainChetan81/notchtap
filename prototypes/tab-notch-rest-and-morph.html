tab-notch proposal 1/2 — rest, icons, selection r3

The notch rests bare: the approved face, and the eq bars when audio is genuinely playing. No icons at rest. Hover widens both flanks and paints them black — the time fades in on the left, and the neon icon-tabs appear inside the right flank, never over the desktop. Clicking an icon selects that tab, and a selected tab means hover also mounts that source's real shipped compact card in the below-block. Expanding it is a separate keystroke, never something hover does for you.

What r3 rejected. (1) r2's permanent strip — the icons floated outside the notch shape over the wallpaper, and that is now impossible by construction. (2) r2's 13px dim icons — 18px now, twice the glow, and all five animate, including weather, which r2 deliberately left still. (3) r2's "one contextual line" — deleted; the below-block shows the shipped compact card instead. (4) r1's click-morph panel stayed dead. Do not restore any of them from git history.

Every curve, colour, radius, and duration below is the shipped one — --ease-notchtap cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1), REVEAL_MS 260ms, HOVER_MS 160ms, 8px radius, #000 shell, the #7fe08d / #ff6b57 / #f0c46a status hue table, --media-mint #b6f5e5, a 200×32 cutout with 1fr flanks, the 85px hover-rail flank, and the 100px below-block (hover.rs IDLE_PEEK_BELOW_BLOCK_H). Only the 18px icon box, the 8px strip gap, and the five glyph drawings are new geometry.

Scope, stated once. Every rig on this page is the Mac mini / HUD presentation — a synthetic notch we draw ourselves, floated under the menu bar. No notch-hardware variants appear here on purpose. And there are no reduced-motion and no accessibility variants anywhere in this file: that is a standing project rule, not an omission to be corrected in review. Every animation below is unconditional.

Originality, stated up front. The face and all five glyphs are original notchtap drawings — not copies of, homages to, or variations on boring.notch's mascot, any icon set (Lucide, SF Symbols, Feather, Meteocons), or any third-party app's character. The face is two .status-dot circles shrunk 9px → 4px plus the 1px hairline .masthead::after already draws. The glyphs are hand-authored paths built from circles, straight strokes and cubics. The weather peek's condition glyph is an original inline drawing standing in for the app's own glyph asset (a raster file this standalone page cannot inline). No third-party logo, asset, vendored path data, or implied affiliation appears anywhere in this file (CLAUDE.md, "naming").

1 — restchanged in r3

What the notch is when nobody is touching it. In r2 this was "face + eq + five icons"; the screenshots showed the icons hanging in mid-air beside the black shape, and that is what r3 deletes. Rest is now the shell, the face, and the eq bars when audio is actually playing. Full stop. The three rigs below are separate boxes, not stacked layers.

a — dead bare

zero app pixels. The honest baseline. Costs nothing, says nothing, impossible to discover.

b — face only approved

two 4px dots + a 1px rule. Breathes on 5.2s; double-blinks every 6.4s, right eye 40ms behind.

c — the proposal at rest r3

face + eq, over a pale desktop. Deliberately the worst-case wallpaper — and there is nothing on it to wash out, because nothing is drawn outside the shell.

The r2 bug, named. r2 sized the resting shell from the strip and left the flanks transparent, so five glowing glyphs were painted directly onto the wallpaper next to a black shape they claimed to belong to. Over a pale desktop the dim ones vanished; over a dark one they read as free-floating dust. Either way they were not part of the notch. r3's rule removes the failure mode rather than tuning it: if the black is not there, the icons are not there.

Chosen default vs logged alternative — we can change this whenever.
CHOSEN: rest-hidden. Nothing but the shell, the face, and the eq bars. Rest costs zero pixels of desktop; discovery is entirely on hover.
ALTERNATIVE, logged not deleted: inside-always. Keep the icons permanently, but give the right flank a permanent black plate so they are always inside the shell — the notch would then rest wider (200px + 2×144px with five sources) and always carry a black shoulder. That buys the free glance r2 was after and pays for it in resting footprint. It is a one-line change to this file's --flank-w rest value plus dropping the strip's visibility gate; it is not a rewrite. Say the word.

live — hover to bring the strip in

Hover the rig. The flanks widen and paint, then the icons arrive inside the right one. Weather and news are there whenever the strip is up; agent, football and music appear only while genuinely live — a session actually running, a match actually in play, audio actually playing. Flip the toggles and watch the hovered shell breathe: each icon costs exactly 26px of flank, and both flanks always move together so the cutout never drifts off centre. Pin hover holds the state open so it can be inspected without a cursor; show flank bounds dashes the right flank's own box so you can see the icons are inside it.

Bigger, because 13px was a rumour of an icon. 13px → 18px is +38%, and at 18px a stroke-only glyph is actually legible as its own shape rather than as a coloured smudge. The strip costs 26px of flank per icon instead of 21px, which is the entire price: the hovered shell with five sources goes 438px → 488px. Since the strip no longer exists at rest, that width is only ever paid while you are looking at it.

Glowier, by stacking two shadows rather than widening one. Every glyph carries drop-shadow(0 0 3px currentColor) drop-shadow(0 0 7px currentColor) — a tight core that keeps the stroke crisp, and a wide bloom that is the actual halo. Widening a single shadow to 10px just blurs the drawing away. Both read currentColor, so hue and glow stay the same single value by construction: when the agent glyph shifts blue to teal, its halo shifts with it for free.

Everything moves now — and that is exactly why they must move differently. r2 kept weather still as the strip's anchor, on the theory that if everything glows nothing does. The operator overruled it. What survives the override is the guard underneath it: the strip is protected from becoming noise by variety instead of stillness. Five motions, five characters, five unrelated periods (1.9s / 2.8s / 900ms / 7.2s / 2.6s) so the strip never lands on a shared downbeat. Weather got the slowest and smallest of them — a 7.2s sway of about a pixel — because it is the one icon that is always there, so it is the one that has to survive being looked at.

Honest cost. The colour-shift keyframes animate color and filter, which are not compositor-only properties, and r3 both enlarged the glyphs and doubled the shadows — so the per-frame repaint is meaningfully more expensive than r2's. At 18px, on at most five glyphs, only while hovered, this should still be invisible; but "only while hovered" is now doing real work in that sentence and it is the reason the cost is acceptable. If profiling ever complains, the fix is two stacked copies of the glyph cross-fading on opacity, not a slower animation. The music bob and the weather sway are transforms and stay on the compositor.

The r2 scrim question is answered by deletion. r2 flagged "the icons glow over the wallpaper, do we need a faint scrim behind the strip?" as an open question. There is no longer anything to scrim: the strip only ever paints on top of a flank that is already #000. One rejected screenshot removed a whole open question.

2 — hover: the flank widens, and the card is the shipped cardchanged in r3

Hover does three things on one clock: both flanks widen and paint #000, the time fades in on the left, and the icon strip arrives inside the right. Beneath all of it, and only if a tab is selected, that source's real shipped compact card. Always compact — hover never expands anything. Hover the notch.

weather selected — hover to mount the shipped weather peek.

one state at a time — rest → hovered → hovered + selected

Three frames in one box. Every frame is display: none by default and exactly one .is-on turns exactly one on — display, not opacity, so there is no moment, mid-transition or otherwise, in which two frames can paint at once. The self-check under the rack proves it from computed style, and proves the two r3 rules alongside it: no icon is ever painted without black behind it, and every visible icon box sits inside its own flank's rect.

The 60ms that fixes the screenshot. The flank's background and the strip's opacity are not on the same beat: the flank starts painting at 0ms, the strip's fade starts at 60ms. So there is no frame in which a glyph is visible against anything but black. Leaving them simultaneous would technically satisfy "the icons are inside the flank" and would still flash the old bug for two frames on a slow paint.

The card is the card we already ship. The weather example above is not a redrawing: it is .below-block.idle-peek at its real 100px, with .wx-peek-backdrop's partly-cloudy mood gradient, .wx-peek-readout (28px condition glyph, 18px mono temperature, the hi/lo pair, the .chip wx-peek-condition label and the .chip wx-peek-rain chip), the three-point .wx-forecast-strip, and the .idle-peek-timeline day dot — the same classes, paddings and type sizes read straight out of src/overlay/idle-peek.css. The weather card is not being changed by this proposal. The only substitution is the condition glyph, which ships as a raster asset this standalone page cannot inline, so an original drawing stands in its slot.

The other four cards are not drawn here. Selecting agent, football, music or news mounts a plate that names the card and points at proposal 2/2 r3, which is the catalogue of all five in compact and expanded form. Drawing four approximations here would mean inventing details that the sibling file would then have to un-invent, and this file's job is the notch, not the cards.

Hover shows COMPACT. Always. There is no "hover long enough and it grows", no hover-intent timer, no second stage. Expansion is a deliberate act with its own keystroke (section 5), so the surface you get by accident is always the small one. That is the single most important behavioural line in this file: hovering is not a request.

Faithfulness caveat. This page drives hover off JS pointer events on the rig. The shipped overlay window is click-through and cannot receive hover, so the real build drives the identical CSS off the rust tracking area's hover-changed event (src-tauri/src/hover.rs → App.tsx). Same styles, different trigger. Note also that a clickable icon strip means the hovered overlay can no longer be unconditionally click-through — though r3 shrinks that problem considerably, since the strip only exists while hovered, and the hover state already has a rust-derived card rect.

3 — selectioncarried from r2

Click an icon to select it. Click it again to deselect. Selecting a second icon moves the selection rather than adding to it — max one, or none. The rig below is pinned hovered with everything live so all five are clickable and the card under them changes as you click.

live — click the icons

nothing selected. click any icon.

  • Max one, or none. A second click on the selected icon clears it; a click on a different icon moves the selection. There is no multi-select and no "sticky last tab" — the empty state is a first-class state, and it is the default.
  • Selection is not opening, and it is not expanding. Selecting a tab decides which compact card hover mounts. It does not open anything on its own, and it never expands anything. Expansion is section 5's keystroke.
  • Selection is now remembered silently, and that is a real cost. In r2 the underline dot survived the cursor leaving, so the resting notch could say "you care about music" in three pixels. r3 removed every resting pixel, so it cannot. The selection is still remembered — hover again and it is exactly where you left it — but there is no longer any way to know what it is without hovering. That is the honest price of a bare rest state, and it is the strongest argument the inside-always alternative in section 1 has.
  • A selection whose icon disappears is cleared, not remembered. Turn "music playing" off while music is selected: the icon collapses and the selection goes with it. Remembering it would mean the notch had a hidden state you could not see or undo — which, with a bare rest state, is now doubly true.
  • Press feedback is on pointer-down. :active scales the icon to 0.9 immediately, before the click commits — the house rule, and the same one every button in the settings window follows.
  • Open question — is the underline dot enough? At 3px it is the smallest mark on the surface and it is doing the heaviest semantic lifting on it. The alternatives are a thin underline bar (reads as a tab, but at 18px wide it is nearly a dot anyway) or letting the brightness change carry it alone (cleaner, but a dim-and-selected icon then looks identical to a live-and-unselected one). Argued for the dot on the grounds that it is a different shape, not just a different weight, and shape survives peripheral vision.

4 — the news chargecarried from r2

News is the one source that is neither live nor idle — it accumulates. Every user-defined cycle (15 minutes, say) fresh items land one at a time; the glyph fills toward the batch size as they do, silently and without a glow. At cycle end, if items are actually waiting, the icon glows and keeps glowing until the tab is visited. Selecting news is what counts as visiting: the glow stops and the fill resets to empty.

empty. batch size 5. run a cycle — the glyph fills, then glows.

Charging and charged are two different things, and conflating them is the trap. Charging is ambient: it happens over fifteen minutes and must never pull the eye, so it is a fill level and nothing else — no glow, no motion, no colour change. Charged is a summons: the cycle has ended, items are genuinely waiting, and now it is allowed to breathe.

r3 makes this quieter than it looks, and someone should decide whether that is right. Both states now live entirely inside the hover strip, so a charged batch summons nobody — you only ever see the glow if you were already hovering. The old always-visible strip was the thing that made "charged" mean anything at a distance. Either that is fine (news is not urgent, and a real story still promotes a card through the normal queue) or the charged state needs its own resting mark, which would be the first crack in the bare-rest rule. Flagged as open question 3.

The fill is a transform, not a height. A rectangle inside the glyph with transform-box: fill-box, a bottom origin, and transform: scaleY(var(--charge)). So the level is one custom property that JS sets and CSS animates on the house curve — the same discipline as the eq bars.

"Visited" means selected, not hovered. Hovering the notch to check the time must not silently mark five unread stories as seen. Only a deliberate click on the news icon clears the glow, which also means the clearing gesture and the reading gesture are the same one.

What the demo compresses. The real cycle is user-defined and measured in minutes; the button above lands five items in about three and a half seconds so the fill is actually watchable. Read the shape of the sequence, not its tempo. The batch size is also fixed at 5 here and would be configuration in the real build.

5 — the keyboard modelnew in r3

A hover-driven surface that you have to reach for with a cursor is a surface you will not use. So the whole of section 2 and 3 gets a keyboard twin: a tmux-style prefix, then one key. Press the prefix, the notch arms, the next keystroke does one thing, and it disarms itself. This is also the only way to expand a card — hover never does it.

  • The prefix is decided: ⌃⇧space, configurable in settings. It has to be a combo nothing else claims, it has to be reachable one-handed, and it must not collide with the seven ⌃⇧ combos above — space is the only key in that family nobody is using. Because it is configurable, a collision with someone's window manager is a setting, not a redesign.
  • Why a prefix at all, instead of seven more direct combos. Direct combos do not scale: five tabs plus session cycling plus expand plus pause is nine bindings, and every one of them has to dodge macOS, the browser, the terminal and whatever else is running. A prefix costs one binding total and buys the entire single-key alphabet after it. It is also the model the people who will actually use this (tmux, vim, emacs users) already have in their fingers.
  • Armed is a real state and it must be visible. A prefix that gives no feedback is a prefix you press twice. See the delight proposal below.
  • Open — what does the armed window do on timeout? Drawn here as 2s, then a silent disarm. The alternative is no timeout at all (armed until you press something), which is what tmux does and which means a stray prefix leaves the notch listening indefinitely. 2s is a guess, not a measurement.
  • Open — should the prefix work when the notch is not hovered? Drawn here as yes: the keystroke summons the surface it needs, so prefix 4 from a full-screen editor selects weather and shows its card without touching the mouse. That is the whole point of a keyboard model, but it does mean a keystroke can make the notch grow while you are looking somewhere else.

Delight proposal — the eyes are the armed indicator. While the prefix is armed, the face's two eyes flick from --overlay-fg to pure white with a 6px halo, and the breath speeds up from 5.2s to 2.2s. Nothing else changes and no text appears anywhere. It is built entirely out of the approved face's own parts — no new element, no badge, no colour that is not already on the surface — so it cannot drift away from the drawing that was approved twice. The creature is listening, and that is the entire message. This is a proposal, not a decision: the conservative alternative is a 1px accent hairline under the cutout, which is duller and would definitely work.

Honest gap. Nothing about this is built. The app registers global shortcuts one combo at a time through tauri_plugin_global_shortcut; a prefix needs a mode — register the prefix globally, then take a temporary key grab for the armed window, then release it. That is a genuinely different mechanism from the seven combos above, not an extension of them, and it should be scoped before it is promised.

6 — open questions

Everything below is genuinely undecided. The glyph drawings in particular are proposals, not a finished set — they are here so there is something concrete to reject.

  • 1. Rest-hidden vs inside-always — the live alternative. This file ships rest-hidden as the default because that is what the operator's rejection asked for, but inside-always is logged, not dead: a permanent black shoulder on the right flank with the strip permanently inside it. It costs about 144px of resting width with five sources and buys back the free glance, the visible selection, and a charged-news state that can actually summon. Switching is a --flank-w rest value plus dropping one visibility gate.
  • 2. The five glyph drawings are proposals. Each is legible at 18px and each has a distinct silhouette from the other four, which was the actual constraint. 18px is kinder to all of them than 13px was. The weakest is still football: a circle with three seam strokes is the honest simplification of a ball, but it sits close to a generic target glyph. The agent prompt-chevron is the most legible and the least specific — it says "a terminal", not "a coding session". Redraw any of them freely.
  • 3. Does a charged news batch need a resting mark? See section 4's warning. With a hover-only strip, "charged" is invisible until you hover, which may make the whole charge model pointless. The options are: accept it (news is not urgent), give charged-news one resting pixel (the first crack in bare rest), or let a charged cycle promote a normal card through the queue and drop the charge model entirely.
  • 4. Fill level, count badge, or both? The news glyph currently shows both: the interior fill (ambient) and an 8px numeral at its top-right (literal). Both are drawn so one can be killed. The numeral eats 6px of the flank's 14px inset, which is the only place on this page where a drawing pokes outside its own box.
  • 5. Two arguable hues. Agent borrows #0a84ff, the app accent — so the strip uses the accent for a source and the accent stops being reserved. Music uses --media-mint #b6f5e5, which is very pale; against a black flank that is now fine, which is one more thing r3's hidden-at-rest rule quietly fixed.
  • 6. Does a clickable strip still break click-through? Much smaller than it was in r2, because the strip only exists while hovered and the hover state already has a rust-derived card rect. But "the overlay must accept clicks in exactly this rect, only while hovered, and go back to click-through the instant hover ends" is still a real piece of rust work, and it should be settled before any of this is planned.
  • 7. What happens on a real-cutout macbook? Out of scope for this page by instruction, but not forever: on a real notch the flanks are the actual screen either side of the physical cutout, next to the menu bar's own right-side items. A 144px black shoulder that appears on hover may collide with the system status items. Someone has to look at it on the macbook.
  • 8. Is five tabs the set? Agent, football, music, weather, news is the current source list and the strip is a 1:1 mirror of it. A sixth source costs another 52px of hovered width and another number on the keymap. There is a size at which this stops working; nobody has found it yet.
